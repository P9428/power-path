## 2. SITE CONTROL IS CONTINGENT — the developer does not own the land

Three independent statements in the record, none of which has been contradicted:

- The annexation *"will take effect upon the developer's acquisition of the
  property."*
- Staff memo, verbatim: *"An initial $20 million will be paid to the City within
  30 days of **closing on the property** and each subsequent phase of development
  will be preceded by a required proportional payment."*
- The petitioner *"intends to annex the 795-acre assemblage"* — an assemblage, i.e.
  multiple parcels, not a single holding.

**Status: the entitlement exists; the ownership does not, on this record.** Whether
PowerHouse Hillwood holds options, purchase agreements, or nothing at all on the
795 acres **is not determinable from the documents assembled here.** See GAPS G1.

This is the land-side instance of the distinction in `SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` §7: an
approval that runs with land the applicant does not yet own is a contractual
position, not a physical one.

## 3. THE POWER PATH — full text of the city's power findings

Staff memo, File #178-26, verbatim and complete:

> "Strain on the local power grid is another primary concern from Data Center
> development. The developer estimates that at full buildout, the proposed project
> would require an **annual electricity consumption of 1,800 megawatts (MW)**.
> ComEd has identified and planned a series of transmission and system upgrades
> that will allow the data center to operate without reducing reliability for
> existing residential or business customers. All required transmission and
> infrastructure improvements associated with the project will be funded by the
> developer. This includes construction of dedicated infrastructure that would
> separate electric service from the local service network that serves existing
> ComEd customers. This isolation from the broader ComEd network would simplify
> service delivery to the development site while preventing complications
> associated with integrating the projected large energy load with the loads of
> existing ComEd customers. The Regional Transmission Organization (RTO) that
> coordinates the movement of wholesale electricity throughout the local grid is
> PJM Interconnection LLC. **PJM must approve all proposed generation assets and
> large loads before they are allowed to connect to the regional grid** to deliver
> or withdraw power. As part of the interconnection process, **PJM and ComEd have
> evaluated the impact of the development at full buildout on the reliability of
> the regional grid and local network and determined that extending service to the
> proposed data center campus site will not cause blackouts to ComEd customers.**
> The development will have backup power generation but will rely on the local
> regional electrical grid for its electricity supply."

And on supply:

> "The proposed data center campus must secure its own electricity supply through
> either the **ComEd Basic Electric Service-Hourly rate** or through a **bilateral
> agreement with a retail electricity supplier**."

### 3a. The physical assets, and who will own them

Staff memo, verbatim:

> "**Electrical substations of approximately 6 acres in size would accompany each
> future phase of development.** A larger, **25-acre switching station** would be
> situated on the development's west side, west of S. Rowell Road. Depicted as
> **Lot 3** on the Preliminary Plat, **this lot would eventually be conveyed to
> ownership by Commonwealth Edison (ComEd).**"

**This is the copper column, and the record answers it.** The largest single
piece of interconnection infrastructure on the site — a 25-acre switching
station — is planned to end up in **ComEd's** ownership, not the campus's. Four
6-acre substations accompany the phases.

Under `SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` §7 the classification of this site's power path is
therefore: **a contractual interconnection right across utility-owned physical
assets**, with the developer funding construction of assets it will convey away.
It is not a physical interconnection asset held by the site.

### 3b. Three things the record does NOT say

Recorded as absences, per the NULL rule — not as doubts, and not softened:

1. **No executed interconnection or service agreement is named anywhere.** The
   memo says PJM *"must approve"* large loads and that PJM and ComEd *"have
   evaluated"* impact. **Evaluation is not approval.** No study name, docket
   number, queue position, or agreement date appears.
2. **Firmness is never stated.** Neither firm nor non-firm service appears in any
   document assembled here. Per §7, a capacity figure without its firmness is not
   a capacity figure.
3. **No date, phasing, or MW-by-phase for electric service.** The buildings are
   phased in four clusters of six; the load is stated only as a full-buildout
   total.

### 3c. A unit error in the document the council voted on

> *"an **annual electricity consumption** of 1,800 **megawatts (MW)**"*

**MW is a rate of power. Annual consumption is energy, measured in MWh or GWh.**
"Annual electricity consumption of 1,800 MW" is not a coherent quantity — the two
halves of the sentence describe different physical dimensions.

Recorded neutrally: the developer's own material says **"1.8-gigawatt (GW)
campus"**, which is a coherent power figure, so the likeliest reading is that the
staff memo mislabels a demand figure as consumption. **It is not corrected here**
— it is recorded as it appears in the primary document, because the document as
written is what the council voted on.

This is the exact conflation `SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md` §7 was written to catch,
appearing unprompted in the governing record of the first site examined.

### 3d. Nearby transmission context — NOT yet tied to this site

ComEd's **Wilton Center 765 kV substation** near Joliet was expanded to enable a
large renewable cluster. **No document assembled here connects the Joliet
Technology Center to Wilton Center.** Recorded as context and as a lead, not as a
finding. See GAPS G3.

## 4. Regulatory exposure that attaches to this site

### 4a. ComEd large-load deposit — ICC Docket 25-0677/25-0679, order 2026-03-19

Tariff formula (see `ASSUMPTIONS.md` C4): **$1,000,000, plus $500,000 for each
additional whole 100 MW of maximum known demand above 200 MW**; deposits at or
over $2,000,000 require an Acceptable Letter of Credit from a U.S. financial
institution.

**Applied to 1,800 MW, stated with its assumptions exposed:**

```
  base                                    $1,000,000
  increments above 200 MW: (1800-200)/100 = 16 whole 100 MW blocks
  16 x $500,000                           $8,000,000
  ------------------------------------------------------
  TOTAL, IF assessed once at full buildout  $9,000,000
  Letter of credit required (> $2,000,000): YES
```

**Assumptions this calculation rests on, every one of them unverified:**
- that the deposit attaches to the **project** rather than per-phase LDPAC
  application — the campus is explicitly four phases;
- that "maximum known demand included in the load ramp" equals the 1,800 MW
  full-buildout figure;
- that the order as issued 2026-03-19 governs, notwithstanding Constellation's
  pending application for rehearing.

**This is arithmetic on a rule, not a finding about this project.** It may not be
presented as the developer's actual exposure. See GAPS G4.

### 4b. PJM Surplus Interconnection Service — why it does NOT apply here

The developer's own material: *"There are no plans for any onsite generation
assets."* The staff memo: the campus *"will rely on the local regional electrical
grid for its electricity supply."*

PJM's tariff limits Surplus Interconnection Service to *"A Generating Facility"*
(see `ASSUMPTIONS.md` C1). **This site is load with backup generation, not a
generating facility, so SIS is unavailable to it as a matter of tariff text.**

Recorded because it forecloses the cheapest hypothetical shortcut: this campus
cannot inherit a retiring generator's interconnection position. It must go
through large-load interconnection, which is what makes §4a bite.

### 4c. FERC EL26-67-000 — live, and directly overhead

FERC's 2026-06-18 show cause order to PJM preliminarily found PJM's tariff
*"appears to be unjust and unreasonable"* as to large-load integration, across
five reform categories including co-location, cost-shifting/transparency, and new
services for flexible large loads. **Response deadline 2026-08-17 — three days
before this dossier.** See GAPS G5: PJM's actual filing was not retrieved.

## 6. The challenge — Doorneweerd v. City of Joliet et al.

- Filed **2026-05-18**, Will County, by counsel for **Joliet Residents For
  Responsible Growth**.
- Named plaintiffs: **Craig Doorneweerd, Rhonda Doorneweerd, Pedro Garcia** — all
  residents of unincorporated **Elwood, Jackson Township**, near the site.
- Defendants include the **City of Joliet, PowerHouse, and Hillwood**.
- Claims, as reported: the rezoning of agricultural land is **unconstitutional**;
  a February public notice failed to disclose that the proposal was a data center
  campus; a March meeting violated the **Open Meetings Act**; residents were
  denied a meaningful opportunity to challenge testimony.
- Also alleged: strain on the aquifer, and power draw compared to Hoover Dam.
- **Judge Victoria Breslan. Hearing 9:00 a.m., 2026-09-08, Will County
  Courthouse** — 19 days after this dossier.

**The complaint itself was not obtained.** All of the above is SECONDARY, from
press. See GAPS G2. **The allegations are recorded as allegations.**

Note for the memo, factually: the water allegation is contested in the record.
Staff memo states the campus *"would utilize a closed-loop system"*, that
*"daily potable water consumption, calculated on an annual basis, will not be
allowed to exceed 150,000 gallons per day,"* and that excess draws pay **3× the
applicable rate**. Whether that resolves the aquifer claim is not a question this
dossier answers.

## 7. What the record permits the memo to conclude

Stated as the boundary of the evidence, not as the conclusion:

- The **classification** of the power path is determinable: contractual right
  across utility-owned copper, with the key 25-acre switching station destined for
  ComEd ownership. §3a.
- The **existence and terms of any interconnection or service agreement** are
  **not** determinable from public record as assembled. §3b.
- **Site control** is **not** determinable. §2, G1.
- Therefore the 30-day kill criterion in `ROADMAP.md` — *"the determination is not
  makeable in principle"* — **does not fire.** The determination was makeable. It
  came back partly resolved and partly NULL, which is a memo, not a failure.
