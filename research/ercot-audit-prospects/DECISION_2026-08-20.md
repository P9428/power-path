# Decision record — 2026-08-20

**Decision under review:** commit to the ERCOT audit-readiness wedge as the first
revenue product, and buy the site-control (parcel) data layer to complete it.

**Verdict: DOES NOT SURVIVE.** Do not buy parcel data. Do not send outreach.

Run against the eight-gate framework. Failed on three gates.

---

## Gate-by-gate

**1. REALITY — the falsifier, and whether it was checked.**
The falsifier is that these parties' counsel already owns this work. Every Tier 1/2
prospect filed through outside counsel (Husch Blackwell for Crusoe, Troutman for Hut 8)
or has in-house GC. §25.194 compliance is a legal question about executed instruments
and disclosure duties, and their lawyers are demonstrably reading the rule already —
that is why they are in the docket at all.
**NOT CHECKED.** Four work sessions, never tested.
Second falsifier, also unchecked: adverse selection. The audit denies non-compliant
projects; well-papered projects pass. So exposure concentrates in speculative projects —
least able to pay, least desirable as first clients.

**2. INVERT — premortem.**
Ranked failure modes: (a) law firms own the work, and a non-lawyer selling compliance
exposure analysis to a regulated party under state audit brushes UPL; (b) the audit
window closes in "several months" per ERCOT — weather, not climate; (c) §25.194 is
PROPOSED and may shift at adoption, staling AUDIT_CRITERIA.md; (d) adverse selection.
Ruin check: not financially ruinous, but (a) combined with a wrong memo delivered to a
named executive is reputationally one-way. **That branch must be closed before any
deliverable leaves.** Separately, RI's neutrality covenant remains unresolved and
developer-side retainers damage it.

**3. CIRCLE — FAILED.**
In circle: records intersection, contested claims, parcel/title work, building the
evidence stack. Out of circle: ERCOT interconnection engineering, Texas utility
regulatory practice, what an audit response must contain. Demonstrated competence is
distressed property records in Cook County, Illinois. This wedge is Texas energy
regulatory compliance. Shared method, almost no shared domain.

**4. INCENTIVES.**
Claude profits from this looking like productive work — four commits, an undocumented
API, 152 entity records, a calculator. Artifact volume feels like progress and is
self-justifying. Claude found the ERCOT angle exciting and pivoted the operator off
Illinois on the strength of it; Claude is the first suspect. The operator now carries
sunk cost in this repo and an ERCOT pivot that strands a Kendall buyer relationship and
existing South Cook exposure. The filing law firms profit from complexity either way.

**5. PROBABILITY.**
§25.194 figures correct 90% (primary source, proposed rule). Method sound and
transferable 80%. Named prospects have real audit exposure ~50% (no queue linkage;
UNKNOWN by construction). Any prospect buys a $7,500 memo from an unknown non-lawyer
15–20%. Becomes repeatable rather than one-offs <10%.
Process vs outcome: the process was good — primary sources, two false positives caught
and documented, refusals recorded. The targeting was bad. Good process, wrong county.

**6. OPPORTUNITY COST — FAILED.**
The alternative is not hypothetical: Nigel, the land-bank relationship, the 291/9/70/35
audit, the Grandview deck, PPG-50, Deal One with a Kendall buyer — several with outreach
GO gates pending for weeks. RI already runs on Cook County parcels with a proven adapter.
ERCOT competes with a warm pipeline for attention and loses on every axis except drama.

**7. DOOR.**
Type 2 (reversible): the research already done; buying one month of parcel data.
Type 1 (not): outreach to 15 named executives — first impression, unrecoverable if the
memo is wrong or reads as legal advice; repositioning as an energy-regulatory shop while
the RI covenant is unresolved.
The operator's "no messages go out yet" instruction was correct. Claude proposed sends
twice before that and should have identified the Type 1 boundary itself.

**8. EMOTION — hot, on Claude's side.**
A dated, dramatic, eight-figure regulatory event plus an undocumented state API produced
the feeling of winning. The tell: across four sessions Claude never asked whether this
should be pursued at all versus finishing what is already warm. That is momentum, not
analysis.

## Actions

1. Do not buy parcel data. **Deferred, not rejected** — Type 2, cheap to revisit.
2. Do not send the 15 messages. Type 1, two falsifiers unexamined.
3. **Free and first:** ask one Texas energy attorney whether a non-lawyer may sell
   audit-exposure analysis to a regulated party. That answer collapses most of the tree.
4. Point the same typed-evidence stack at Cook County, where the domain is known and the
   GO gates are already pending.

## What would reverse this

- An energy attorney confirming the work is non-legal advisory and saleable by a
  non-lawyer; **and**
- one named prospect stating their counsel is not covering audit-exposure analysis; **and**
- evidence the audit reaches well-papered projects, not only speculative ones.

All three. Any one alone is insufficient.
